5 Warning Signs a Semi Truck DPF Needs Immediate Attention

A DPF or aftertreatment problem can start as a warning light and turn into a derate, lost power, or an out-of-service truck. These are five signs worth addressing before the problem gets worse.

Commercial Truck Restoration & Repair · Cliffwood, New Jersey · September 2026

Modern diesel aftertreatment systems depend on the diesel particulate filter (DPF) to capture soot from the exhaust stream. As soot accumulates, the system periodically performs regeneration to burn much of that material off. When regeneration is not completing properly, soot loading can continue to rise and the truck may begin displaying warnings or limiting performance.

Not every DPF light means the filter itself has failed. Sensors, wiring, exhaust leaks, fuel-system problems, DEF/SCR faults, operating conditions, and other engine issues can all contribute to aftertreatment problems. The goal is to diagnose the cause instead of repeatedly clearing codes or forcing regens without understanding why the problem keeps returning.

1. The DPF Warning Light Is Staying On or Flashing

A DPF lamp that remains illuminated is an early warning that the system is asking for regeneration. A flashing DPF lamp generally indicates a higher soot load and should be taken more seriously. If the DPF lamp is flashing together with a check-engine or warning lamp, the truck may require immediate regeneration or repair.

Do not ignore an escalating DPF warning. Continued operation can push the system farther into restriction and may eventually lead to a severe derate or stop-engine condition.

2. The Truck Keeps Asking for Regeneration

An occasional regeneration is normal. A truck that repeatedly requests parked regens, fails to complete them, or needs them much more often than expected may have an underlying issue.

Possible causes include excessive soot production, failed temperature or pressure sensors, exhaust leaks, injector or engine problems, or an aftertreatment fault preventing the regeneration process from completing correctly.

Commercial Truck Restoration & Repair performs electronic and electrical diagnostics and can evaluate fault codes and operating data rather than simply treating the warning light as the problem.

3. The Truck Is Losing Power or Beginning to Derate

As soot loading or aftertreatment faults become more serious, the engine control system may reduce available power or vehicle speed. Drivers may notice weak acceleration, difficulty maintaining highway speed, reduced throttle response, or an obvious programmed derate.

A derate should be treated as a symptom that requires diagnosis. The problem may involve the DPF, differential-pressure readings, exhaust-temperature sensors, DEF dosing, SCR performance, or another engine or aftertreatment fault.

4. Check-Engine and Aftertreatment Codes Keep Returning

Clearing a fault code does not repair the condition that triggered it. If aftertreatment, DPF, DEF, NOx-sensor, temperature-sensor, or pressure-sensor codes continue returning, the system should be tested before the truck is put back into regular service.

Our shop works on Detroit Diesel, Cummins, Volvo/Mack, and PACCAR-powered trucks and handles DPF and aftertreatment diagnostics, DEF/SCR diagnostics, forced or parked regeneration, and DPF replacement when replacement is actually required.

5. A Regen Will Not Complete

A parked or forced regeneration that aborts, will not initiate, or repeatedly fails is a strong reason to stop guessing. The engine control module may be preventing regeneration because another active fault is present, or the system may not be reaching the temperatures and operating conditions necessary to complete the process.

Before replacing expensive aftertreatment components, the truck should be properly diagnosed. Depending on the fault, the solution may involve a sensor, wiring repair, engine repair, DEF/SCR component, regeneration issue, or replacement of the DPF itself.

Regeneration and DPF Cleaning Are Not the Same Thing

Regeneration burns accumulated soot. Physical DPF cleaning removes noncombustible ash that remains in the filter over time. Commercial Truck Restoration & Repair performs diagnostics, regeneration-related service, and DPF replacement, but we do not offer off-truck DPF cleaning.

Get the Cause Diagnosed Before It Becomes a Bigger Repair

If your truck is showing DPF warnings, repeatedly requesting regeneration, losing power, or beginning to derate, diagnosing the underlying cause early can help avoid additional downtime and unnecessary parts replacement.
